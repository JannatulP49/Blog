 I ROBOT BLOG



<p>The movie I, Robot depicts a world where robots and artificial intelligence exist and perform everyday functions such as transportation, manual labor and decision-making process. The main character in I, Robot is Detective Spooner and he investigates the murder of a scientist and begins to suspect that a robot has committed it. However, robots should not be able to commit crimes under The Three Laws of Robotics, this calls into question the assumption that AI is always “safe” from unintended consequences. As AI is represented in movies like I, Robot, this representation will have an impact on how people perceive AI technology in the real world when it comes to trust or fear about what AI can do. Ultimately this fear will lead people to reject any new advancements in AI, thereby impeding the further development of our society.
</p>

<br>

<p>One significant example of how AI is portrayed is the Three Laws of Robotics and how they end up conflicting. The laws are supposed to protect humans, but the main AI system, VIKI, decides that controlling humans is the best way to keep them safe. This shows how AI decision-making can go wrong when a system follows logic without fully understanding human values. Another example is Sonny, the robot who shows emotions, imagination, and independent thinking. He dreams and questions his purpose, which is not very realistic for today’s AI, but it works as a symbol to make the audience think about identity and what it means to be human. A third example is how much society depends on robots for daily life. People trust them to drive cars, protect them, and manage systems, which shows both the convenience of AI and the risks of relying on it too much.
</p>

<p>
Different characters in the film have very different perspectives about robots. For instance, Spooner does not trust robots after an incident in which a robot made a decision based on pure logic that ended with him being saved rather than a child. The rest of the characters in this particular film, however, seem to be much more trusting of robots and artificial intelligence than Spooner. This is a realistic representation of how real-world individuals have vastly different opinions on AI. A large majority of the population will view AI as a valuable resource while others will express concerns over privacy violations, bias, and loss of control.
</p>

<p>
Personally, my views on AI are somewhere in between these two perspectives. Unlike Spooner, I generally trust AI tools like ChatGPT because they can be helpful for learning, answering questions, and supporting everyday tasks. However, I also wouldn’t feel comfortable sharing sensitive personal information or relying on AI to make serious real-world decisions. The movie reinforced my belief that AI should be used as a tool, not something with full control over human lives. It made me think more critically about the importance of limits and human oversight when it comes to advanced technology. 
</p>

<p>
Overall, this movie does a good job of showing how some people are going to think AI is good while other people will think AI is bad. The movie points to the need for clear guidelines and restrictions on how we can use AI, so it can be used in a manner that will help us, but in a safe manner. There are also serious ethical issues raised in this movie regarding who is in control of AI and how much control AI will have over the user and how we as humans accept responsibility for AI's actions. This portrayal of AI can greatly influence how society views AI, through making people more aware of how AI is being developed in real life.
</p>
